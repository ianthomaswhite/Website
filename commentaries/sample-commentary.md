<p>
Notes and structural commentary on Saussure's foundational 1879 treatise establishing the algebraic necessity of laryngeal coefficients in Proto-Indo-European ablaut.
</p>

<div class="commentary-block">
    <div class="commentary-ref">Chapter II &middot; Section 1 &middot; Page 14</div>
    <div class="commentary-quote">
        "Nous nous proposons de prouver que l'a indoeuropéen n'est pas homogène, mais qu'il se subdivise en deux phonèmes distincts dès l'origine..."
    </div>
    <div class="commentary-body">
        <p>
            Here Saussure initiates the structuralist revolution. Rather than treating irregular vowel alternations as phonetic accidents, he postulates latent structural phonemes (what would later be termed $*h_1, *h_2, *h_3$).
        </p>
    </div>
</div>

<div class="commentary-block">
    <div class="commentary-ref">Chapter III &middot; Section 4 &middot; Page 42</div>
    <div class="commentary-quote">
        "Ces coefficients sonantiques fonctionnent exactement comme les liquides et les nasales..."
    </div>
    <div class="commentary-body">
        <p>
            The crucial insight: laryngeals pattern distributionally identically to sonorants ($*r, *l, *m, *n$), forming diphthongs when preceded by $*e$, and vocalizing in zero-grade interconsonantal environments.
        </p>
    </div>
</div>
